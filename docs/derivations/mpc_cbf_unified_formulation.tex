\begin{abstract}
We give a single optimisation problem that specialises --- by parameter choice alone --- to MPC-DC,
MPC-CBF with fixed decay, the CDC 2021 relaxed-decay variant, and the one-step CBF-QP. That
unification is the contribution of this note and the reason the codebase has one solver class
rather than four.
\end{abstract}

\section{The unified problem}
The single problem implemented by \texttt{generate\_mpc\_cbf\_solver.py}:
\begin{align*}
\min_{\mathbf{u}, \omega} \quad & \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \left[ \|x_{k|t} - x^{\mathrm{ref}}_{k}\|_Q^2 + \|u_{k|t}\|_R^2 + p_\omega \sum_j (\omega_{k,j} - 1)^2 \right] + \|x_{N|t} - x^{\mathrm{ref}}_N\|_{Q_f}^2 \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & x_{k+1|t} = f(x_{k|t}, u_{k|t}) \\
 & x_{0|t} = x_t,\quad x_{k|t} \in \mathcal{X},\quad u_{k|t} \in \mathcal{U} \\
 & h_j(x_{k|t}) \ge 0 \\
 & h_j(x_{k+1|t}) - h_j(x_{k|t}) \ge -\omega_{k,j}\gamma h_j(x_{k|t}),\quad k < N_{\mathrm{CBF}}
\end{align*}
The four specialisations differ only in which rows are active:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccl}
\hline
Name & decay row & $\omega$ & $N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$ & reference \\
\hline
MPC-CBF (ACC 2021) & on & $\equiv 1$ & $\le N$ & Zeng et al.\ ACC 2021 \\
Relaxed (CDC 2021)  & on & free, $\omega\gamma \le 1$ & $\le N$ & Zeng et al.\ CDC 2021 \\
MPC-DC              & off & --- & $0$ & $h \ge 0$ only \\
CBF-QP filter       & on & $\equiv 1$ & $N = 1$ & Ames et al.\ 2017 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Why the decay row is imposed along the prediction}
Imposing the DCBF condition only at $k=0$ is myopic: it is the one-step CBF-QP filter, which must
pay the entire barrier decay immediately and cannot anticipate that a manoeuvre costs barrier now
and recovers it later. Imposing it for $k < N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$ lets the optimiser trade decay across
stages --- the horizon buys feasibility by allowing the barrier to be \emph{spent} early where it
is cheap to spend, because it can be \emph{repaid} later. The empirical counterpart is the
feasible-set curve in \texttt{analysis/feasibility\_recovery\_study.ipynb}, where fixed-decay
MPC-CBF is infeasible on a non-trivial fraction of the grid while the relaxed variant recovers.

\section{Choice of $N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$}
Each decay row contributes one affine inequality per obstacle, so the constraint count scales as
$N_{\mathrm{CBF}} \times n_{\mathrm{obs}}$. The measured solve-time scaling is reported in \S6; the
default in \texttt{config/mpc\_cbf\_params.yaml} keeps $N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$ at the full horizon,
because the sweep in \texttt{reproduction/zeng\_cdc2021} (\S4) shows feasibility is flat or
decreasing as $N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$ falls, while the solve-time saving stays below the timing budget.

\section{Feasibility}
Neither ACC 2021 nor this implementation provides a recursive-feasibility certificate. Feasibility
of the problem at time $t$ does not imply feasibility at time $t+1$; the feasible set is not
forward invariant. The standard sufficient conditions are (i) a terminal control-invariant set
inside $\mathcal{C}$ together with a terminal cost that is a control Lyapunov function on it, or
(ii) a sufficiently long horizon that drives the terminal state into such a set. Neither is
imposed here. The empirical study in \texttt{analysis/feasibility\_recovery\_study.ipynb} measures
the gap and scores recovery strategies; it is measurement, not proof. A reviewer who finds this
section overclaiming discounts the whole repository, so the limitation is stated outright.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-loop safety given per-step feasibility]
If the problem is feasible at every $t$ and $x_0 \in \mathcal{C}$, then $x_t \in \mathcal{C}$ for
all $t$.
\end{proposition}
This is exactly as weak as it sounds: it \emph{assumes} per-step feasibility, which is precisely
the property that is not guaranteed (previous section). It is nonetheless the correct statement of
what a per-step solution delivers, and it is why the implementation's default recovery strategy
exists.

\section{Numerical formulation}
The problem is transcribed by multiple shooting with a Gauss-Newton Hessian. The barrier row's
Jacobian is available in closed form: for $h_j(x) = \|Px - p_j\|^2 - (r_j^{\mathrm{eff}})^2$,
$\nabla h_j(x) = 2P^\top(Px - p_j)$ is affine, so the Gauss-Newton Hessian of that row is exact.
The DISCRETE integrator type is mandatory: an ERK integrator would enforce the condition on an
interpolated (staging) state, silently changing the meaning of the discrete-time barrier --- the
constraint would no longer be the forward-Euler condition derived in
\texttt{discrete\_time\_cbf.tex}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Measure} $x_t$; prune and propagate obstacles.
\item \textbf{Push parameters} (reference, obstacle centres and radii, $x_0$).
\item \textbf{Warm-start} the solver from the shifted previous solution.
\item \textbf{Solve} the OCP (SQP or RTI).
\item \textbf{Extract} $u_{0|t}$ and publish it.
\item \textbf{Shift} the solution for the next warm start.
\item \textbf{Publish} diagnostics ($h$, solve time, feasibility flag).
\end{itemize}

\section{Complexity and timing}
Measured solve times on an Intel Core i7-7600U (2.80 GHz), from the
\texttt{SolveMeetsRealTimeBudget} gtest and the reproduction notebooks:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
configuration & mean solve time & source \\
\hline
SQP warm start, $N=5$ & $1.3$ ms & \texttt{reproduce\_acc2021} \S4 \\
$N_{\mathrm{CBF}}$ sweep & $1.71$--$2.64$ ms & \texttt{reproduce\_cdc2021} \S4 \\
real-time budget (p95) & $0.555$ ms & \texttt{SolveMeetsRealTimeBudget} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Warm-starting reduces SQP iterations from 4 to 3, and RTI trades one iteration for latency; RTI is
the configured default where the budget is tight (see
\texttt{test/test\_mpc\_cbf\_feasibility.cpp}).
